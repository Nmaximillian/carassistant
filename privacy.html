Car Assistant – Privacy Policy

Last updated: 21 November 2026 (voice and research services, caches, location, test recordings, deletion)

Car Assistant is a private, non-commercial voice assistant for a single household's car. It is not offered to the public. This page explains what data it uses, where it goes and how long it is kept. "The household's server" is a computer the household runs itself.

Voice and questions

  • Listening for the wake phrase and checking who is speaking happen on the phone.
  • Once the speaker is recognised, what they say is sent to OpenAI's Realtime API, which understands the request and speaks the answer. Results of the lookups it asks for (for example calendar items or the weather) are passed to it for that answer.
  • General-knowledge questions, explanations, advice and questions needing current information ("research") may be sent through the household's server to Anthropic's Claude service, which may answer from its own knowledge or search and open public web pages. Approximate location and country go with them (see Location). The assistant is instructed to leave out personal details and calendar or email content, except a place or address needed to answer the question.
  • Under OpenAI's and Anthropic's API terms, data sent through their APIs is not used to train their models unless the account holder opts in; that is not the same as not keeping it at all (see OpenAI and Anthropic). Car Assistant never uses Google data or conversations for advertising or profiling. Diagnostic information and the optional test recordings are used to troubleshoot and improve the assistant, as described below.

Google account data

When a household member links a Google account, the app requests read-only access to:

  • Google Calendar (calendar.readonly): to answer spoken questions such as "what's on my calendar tomorrow?" or "where is my booking?".
  • Gmail (gmail.readonly): to answer spoken questions about the person's own emails, such as booking confirmations.
  • The account's email address (openid, email): to show which account is linked.

The app never creates, changes, sends or deletes calendar events or emails.

  • Calendar and email data is read only when the verified account holder asks a question, and only the items needed for the answer (for example event title, time and location) are used. They are passed to OpenAI to speak the answer, as described above.
  • The household's server keeps each lookup's result in its memory for up to 10 minutes, so a retried request isn't looked up twice. It is never written to disk.
  • So that follow-up questions work ("how far is it from there?"), the phone keeps a short recap of recent conversations, including the details that were looked up, in its memory only (never saved to storage). It is used only for the same person, and sent to OpenAI at the start of that person's next conversation. Entries older than a period the user chooses (5 hours by default) are removed when the recap is next used or updated. Tapping Stop or closing the app's screen does not necessarily clear it; it is cleared when the app's process ends, or with "Forget now" or "Delete everything".
  • Google user data is not sold. The details needed for an answer are shared with OpenAI. When a requested place search, research answer or navigation needs it, a place or address from those details may also go to the service described below (Google Places, Claude or Google Maps).

Location

If location sharing is on and the phone has a fix, its location goes with every request the phone makes to the household's server for a lookup; how precise it is depends on the app's location setting and the permission given. The server keeps no location history, though lookup results that depend on location can stay in the temporary caches described above. It is passed on to the service doing the lookup: the weather services Open-Meteo and Buienradar (rounded to about 1 km), Google Places for nearby places, and Claude for research questions (approximately). "Where am I?" uses Android's address lookup, which may use the network, and the resulting place is passed to OpenAI to say the answer. Saved places (names and addresses) are kept in the app's private storage on the phone until removed. Their names are given to the assistant, and a destination is handed to Google Maps to navigate.

Music

To play music, the household's server sends search words from the request (for example "Adele Hello") to YouTube's search service (YouTube Data API), using the app's own API key: no Google account, location or audio is sent with it. The phone then opens the song in YouTube Music, or, if nothing was found, passes the search words to YouTube Music. YouTube Music works under its own settings and privacy policy. The server keeps the search result in memory for up to 10 minutes and never writes it to disk or its logs. The phone's recap (above) may remember what was played.

What is stored on the household's server

  • For a linked Google account: its email address, a label ("personal" / "work"), and Google's refresh token, encrypted, used only to read the scopes listed above.
  • Profiles: an identifier, display name, role, whether it is enabled, and which tools it may use. Voice samples recorded to set up a profile are processed on the phone and then discarded; the resulting voiceprints, used to recognise who is speaking, stay encrypted on the phone and are never uploaded.
  • The phone's registration, its settings and consent records.
  • Audit records (what was done and when; no message, calendar or voice content). Database records older than 30 days are removed by a periodic cleanup. The server's own operating logs may contain the same entries.

Optional test-drive features (all off by default)

  • Send the Diagnostics log to my server: the app's diagnostic log (states, timings, voice-check scores, error messages, and short snippets of recognised words when a wake phrase was missed or during wake and pronunciation tests) is stored on the household's server. No audio. Error messages are stored as they are; they are not meant to contain conversations, calendar or email content, but they are not filtered. A day's file is removed by a periodic cleanup once seven days have passed since the file was last written.
  • Wake test log: once switched on, it stays on every time the assistant listens, also after Stop and app restarts, until it is switched off or "Delete everything" is used. While on, up to the first 4 recognised words of each thing the phone hears while waiting for the wake phrase are added to the diagnostic log, including what passengers say and the start of a question asked when the wake phrase was missed. Words only, no audio; not the answers, and not questions asked after the assistant woke. They reach the household's server only if "Send the Diagnostics log" is also on.
  • Share diagnostic events with my server: state changes, timings and voice-check scores, without audio or transcripts, go to the server's operating logs.
  • Record test audio: needs the phone's fingerprint or screen lock to switch on. While on, what the phone's microphone hears during listening is recorded: questions, the assistant's spoken answers, passengers, music and the radio, so everyone in the car should be told. The recordings are used, on the household's own computer, to develop and test wake-phrase detection, speech detection and the voice check, including training the household's own wake-phrase model. Recording stops at Stop, after 30 minutes of audio, or when the app restarts; pieces already recorded may still be uploaded after that. The audio goes in pieces of up to a minute to the household's server only; this feature never sends it to OpenAI, Anthropic or anyone else. Until a piece is uploaded it waits encrypted on the phone; pieces older than a day are removed there when the app starts or saves or sends a piece. On the server, recordings are removed by a periodic cleanup 14 days after they arrived.

Your control

  • Delete everything (in the app, Setup → Privacy) deletes all profiles and voiceprints, saved places, the recap, the device's signing keys and settings on the phone, and unsent test recordings. It asks the household's server to delete this phone's diagnostic log lines, test recordings, settings, consent and audit records; that needs a connection, and the app says if it failed. It does not unlink Google accounts: unlink them first (below).
  • Unlink an account in the app (Voices → Unlink) deletes the stored link and token from the server and asks Google to revoke the access. Deleting a person's profile does the same for all of their linked accounts. Both need a connection to the server, and Google's revocation can fail, so to be sure, also check myaccount.google.com/permissions, where access can be revoked at any time.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Car Assistant's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
